## Small orders

For positive odd w the final order equals 48w+8 and is congruent to 56 modulo 96. Below 10,000 this restricts w to at most 207. Replaying the named-source arithmetic screen reproduced its frozen table byte for byte. Selecting the order-12 transfer and final orders below 10,000 gives [79 distinct orders with their witnesses](orders-below-10000.tsv). All were already recorded as known. For the applicable symmetric Paley cores the table identifies Seberry–Yamada’s Corollary 31 directly. The 27 obtained specifically from Turyn or Spence with a Paley conference use prime powers q congruent to 1 modulo 4 and w=qʳ(q+1)/2 with r nonnegative, requiring 6w+1 to be a prime power as well. The scalar input w=1 yields 56. Other named Williamson-type or conference constructions supply the balance. This finite list reports positive witnesses from the screened sources.

- **5,912** is the one direct overlap among the 79 that had exponent greater than 3 in the 2025 Cati–Pasechnik Table 4 and has a documented new construction since August 2026. Kumar gives a Spence construction over a group of order 1477, with a dated October 28 commit and an October 29 main-history addition. Here w=123. In Seberry and Yamada’s Corollary 31 take the prime 61, congruent to 1 modulo 4. Then 2·61−1=121 is a prime power, so the four Williamson-type matrices have order 2·61+1=123, explicitly listed in Remark 32. The pairing gives order 1476, and the Paley conference at 739 completes the transfer. [Corollary 31 and Remark 32, p. 122](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2007/vol-007-paper%206.pdf) **8,696** also appears on the November survey's Table 5, but the 1992 Seberry–Yamada table already supplies it through Williamson-type order 543. Its 2026 implementation recovers an older order. - **Sporadic orders.** The 29 entries in the “Sporadic (computer search)” row of Egan–Kotsireas–London–Ó Catháin's Table 4 are all 4 modulo 8. None can be a direct final order here. The historical order 428 is also 4 modulo 8 and is not divisible by 12. Three entries in the survey row are divisible by 12: 2148, 2292 and 3732. The corresponding Williamson-type orders 179, 191 and 311 do not occur among the screened sources. The paired intermediate family does include 2076 and 9708, two orders treated elsewhere in the survey: the inputs at 173 and 809 use Miyamoto's Williamson-type construction with a symmetric conference of order 86 and a symmetric Hadamard of order 404, respectively. These are comparisons of orders, without claims of equivalence between matrices.
